Privacy Policy for WebDAV QS

Effective date: 27 September 2026

WebDAV QS is a macOS app provided by Tony Scott. It lets you share folders you choose on your Mac with devices that connect through a web browser or a compatible WebDAV app. It does not require an account with me or a cloud service operated by me.

Information handled by the app

On your Mac, WebDAV QS stores the users you create, including their names, passwords and access settings, in the macOS Keychain. It stores your shared-folder choices, folder locations, settings and the network addresses of devices you approve in the app's local settings. If you use HTTPS, the app also keeps its certificate and private key in the Keychain.

When a device connects, the app handles its network address, the username and password it supplies, and its file requests. The Activity panel shows recent requests, including the username where known, network address, requested path, result and amount transferred. This list is held in memory and is cleared when the app quits. If you turn on Save to a log file, the same kind of activity is also written to CSV files on your Mac. The app deletes CSV logs older than 30 days when it next opens. Passwords are not written to the activity list or log files.

If you allow notifications, macOS may show connection approvals and security alerts. An approval notification can include a username and the connecting device's network address. You can turn notifications off in the app's settings.

The files you choose to share remain in their original folders. While sharing is on, signed-in devices can receive file and folder names, previews and file contents according to the access you give them. If you allow uploads for a user, that user can add new files to the folders you gave them. A device may keep copies of files it downloads; WebDAV QS cannot remove those copies.

Network connections and security

File-sharing traffic goes directly between your Mac and the devices that connect to it. WebDAV QS does not automatically send your files, credentials or activity records to me or to a developer-operated server. It has no advertising or analytics SDKs and does not track users across apps or websites.

Access is limited to devices with local-network addresses by default. A device reaching your Mac through a private network or VPN can also count as local. You can change this setting for a user if you choose to arrange wider network access yourself. New devices require your approval by default. You can stop sharing at any time.

HTTP is the default connection method because many media players support it. HTTP does not encrypt passwords or files in transit. Optional HTTPS encrypts the connection, but it uses a certificate created by the app, so browsers may show a trust warning and some media players may not connect. If you enable Bonjour discovery, the app advertises its availability to devices on your local network.

Your choices and deletion

You can change or remove users and shared folders in the app, forget approved devices in Settings › Security, clear the Activity panel, and turn off file logging. Show Files in the Activity panel opens the log folder so you can delete saved logs. Files in the folders you shared, including files uploaded by other users, remain on your Mac until you delete them yourself.

Deleting the app does not necessarily remove its Keychain items, local settings or log files. For complete removal, delete the remaining WebDAV QS items in Keychain Access and the app's local data and logs on your Mac. If you need help finding them, contact me at the address below. Copies already downloaded by other devices must be removed on those devices.

Other services and support

Apple handles App Store purchases and any diagnostics or usage information collected through its services under Apple's Privacy Policy. Browser, file and media apps that connect to WebDAV QS are separate apps with their own privacy practices.

If you read this policy on GitHub Pages, GitHub may log your network address when you visit the page for security purposes, as described in GitHub's privacy information. This is separate from using WebDAV QS to share files.

If you email me for support, I receive your email address and the information you choose to send. I use it to answer your enquiry. My legal basis for this is my legitimate interest in providing support. I keep the correspondence only for as long as needed to handle it, unless a longer period is legally required.

You can contact me to ask what support correspondence I hold about you, to correct it, to object to its use, or to request its deletion. If you are in the UK and are unhappy with how a privacy concern is handled, you can also contact the Information Commissioner's Office.
